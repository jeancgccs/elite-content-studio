# Platform Reference

Per-platform specs, culture, and discovery mechanics. Specs drift over time —
when precision matters (exact character limits, new features), verify with a
quick search if tools are available, and say when a number may be stale.

## YouTube (long-form)

- 16:9, thumbnail 1280×720 (<2 MB), title ≤100 chars (aim ≤60), description
  5,000 chars, chapters need 0:00 + ≥3 stamps.
- Discovery: browse (home) + suggested dominate mature channels; search sustains
  evergreen; CTR × satisfaction-weighted watch time drives recommendations.
- Culture: depth wins; regular cadence and consistent formats build habitual viewers.
- Ideal lengths: whatever retention supports — 8–15 min typical for education;
  20–40+ min works when the format earns it (documentaries, deep dives).

## YouTube Shorts

- 9:16, ≤3 min (sweet spot 20–40s), separate Shorts shelf/feed.
- Discovery: swipe-away rate in the first seconds, completion/loop, shares.
- Shorts→long-form conversion is weak but nonzero: use the "related video" link,
  channel-consistent branding, and topic continuity to help it.

## TikTok

- 9:16; culture prizes authenticity, speed, trends, and native text/sounds —
  polished "ad-like" content underperforms.
- Discovery: interest graph, not follower graph; every post can hit the For You
  page. Early completion + rewatch + share decide reach. Trending sounds and
  formats give a real (short-lived) boost — join fast or skip.
- Caption: short hook + 3–5 relevant hashtags (mix one broad, several niche).
  On-screen text is indexed — put the keyword in the video itself.
- Safe zones: keep text away from right rail (icons) and bottom ~25% (caption UI).

## Instagram Reels

- 9:16; leans slightly more aesthetic/polished than TikTok, same hook physics.
- Discovery: Reels tab + Explore; **shares and saves** are the power metrics.
- Caption: hook line first (grid truncates early), 3–8 hashtags, keyword-rich —
  Instagram search reads captions.
- Same safe zones as TikTok. Cover image matters for the profile grid.

## Instagram (carousels & Stories)

- Carousels: up to 20 slides, 4:5 portrait; strongest save/share format for
  educational content; slide 1 is a thumbnail — design it like one.
- Stories: 24h, casual; polls/questions/sliders drive the interaction signals
  that keep you at the front of the Stories row; link stickers for traffic.

## Facebook

- Native video (feed + Reels) strongly preferred over links; 1:1 or 4:5 for feed.
- Discovery: shares and meaningful comment threads; Groups are the organic-reach
  stronghold. Older-skewing demographics in many regions — adjust tone/topics.

## LinkedIn

- Native video ≤10 min (1–3 min sweet spot), square/vertical fine; documents
  (carousel PDFs) are a top organic format.
- First 2 lines decide the "see more" click. Professional storytelling — lessons,
  numbers, process — outperforms promotion. Comments (especially early, and
  author replies) drive distribution; external links are best in first comment
  or, if in-post, expect reduced reach.
- ≤3 hashtags; posting 1–2×/weekday is plenty.

## X (Twitter)

- Punchy, opinionated, thread-friendly. Native video and images boost reach;
  external links tend to underperform — thread the value, link in the last post
  or a reply.
- Discovery: early engagement velocity (replies, reposts, dwell). Hooks matter
  as much as anywhere: first line = title.
- Hashtags largely unnecessary (0–1); they read as dated on X.

## Threads

- Conversational, lower-stakes than X; text-first with images/video fine.
- Discovery: replies and topic tags; the algorithm favors conversation starters.
  Ask-style and hot-take-with-substance posts travel best. No link penalty
  paranoia needed, but value-first still wins.

## Pinterest

- 2:3 vertical pins (1000×1500); a **search engine**: keyword the pin title,
  description, and board names. Content half-life is months-to-years — best for
  evergreen how-to, design, food, DIY, finance niches.

## Podcasts

- Discovery is weak in-app (search + charts + recommendations); growth comes from
  guesting, clips on video platforms, and the host's other channels.
- Publish video versions to YouTube (full + clips); write keyword-rich episode
  titles (guest name + topic promise, not just "Ep. 47").
- Show notes: timestamps, links, guest bios — they're the SEO surface.
- Podcast-primary creator? Read `references/podcast-first.md` — full playbook
  (clip machine, episode packaging, consumption-rate retention, interview craft).

## Newsletters (email)

- The only audience channel the creator owns. Growth: content upgrades (the
  checklist/template from the video), embedded signup moments, cross-promo.
- Metrics: open rate (subject + sender trust), click rate (one clear CTA),
  reply rate (ask questions — replies improve deliverability).

## Blogs

- See `publishing-seo.md` for on-page SEO. Role in the system: search surface
  Google can rank, embed host for videos, and permanent home for resources.

## Channel art (banners)

Banner crops vary by device more than any other asset — design for the safe
zone, and verify current specs before final export (these drift):

- **YouTube**: upload 2560×1440, but only a centered ~1546×423 strip is visible
  on every device (TV shows the full canvas; mobile crops hardest). All text,
  logos, and schedule info go inside that strip; the rest is decorative bleed.
- **X (Twitter)**: 3:1 header (1500×500); the profile photo overlaps the lower
  left — keep that corner clear.
- **LinkedIn**: ~4:1 banner (1584×396 personal); the profile photo overlaps the
  lower left on desktop and shifts on mobile — keep key content right-of-center.
- **Facebook**: Page cover displays ~820×312 on desktop but crops to roughly
  640×360 on mobile — the sides crop hardest, so keep critical content centered.
- When a platform's spec is uncertain, the general rule: keep critical content
  in the central 70–80% and let the edges be decorative.
- Generating banner art with AI: leave the safe zone as negative space and
  overlay all type separately — the no-text rule in `ai-toolkit.md` applies.

## Livestreams (YouTube/Twitch/TikTok Live)

- Discovery is modest; streams deepen existing community rather than acquire.
  Schedule consistency, a titled agenda (streams need packaging too), interaction
  rituals, and a plan to clip 2–4 highlights into Shorts/videos afterward.
