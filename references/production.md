# Phase 5: Production Direction

Direct like a DP and producer who respects the creator's actual gear and time.
Always ask (or use known context for): camera(s), lenses, lights, mics, location,
crew (usually none), and hours available. Give **two tiers** when uncertain:
"with what you have" and "one upgrade that matters most".

## Camera & framing

- **A-cam talking head**: eye-level or very slightly above; subject offset on a
  thirds line facing into the open side; ~1–2m from background for separation.
  Suggest focal-length feel: 35mm-equivalent for energy/context, 50–85mm for
  flattering compression in sit-down pieces.
- **Coverage plan**: for each script section, specify shot size (wide/medium/CU),
  angle changes for pattern interrupts (a second angle — even the same camera
  moved — doubles editing flexibility), and insert shots.
- **Movement**: motivated moves only. Slow push-ins on emotional beats; handheld
  or gimbal for process/action; locked-off for teaching clarity.
- **Phone-first creators**: rear camera, 4K, exposure locked, gridlines on,
  clean the lens — cost-free upgrades that beat most gear purchases.
- Specialty options when relevant: overhead rig for hands-on demos, screen
  recordings (record at highest resolution, zoom in post), POV/action cam for
  process immersion, drone establishing shots (check local rules), 360 cam for
  set tours or interactive content.

## Lighting

- Base recipe: **key light** 45° off-axis, slightly above eye line, softened
  (softbox/diffusion/window); **fill** from reflector or dim source opposite;
  **back/hair light** or practical lights (lamps, LED strips) for depth.
- Window light is a free key light — face the window, camera between window and subject.
- Consistency beats fanciness: same lighting setup every video builds visual brand.
- Mood mapping: bright + even = trustworthy/educational; contrasty + warm
  practicals = cinematic/story; colored accent (one hue max) = tech/gaming energy.

## Audio (priority #1 — viewers forgive soft video, never bad audio)

- Mic hierarchy by budget: lav or USB dynamic close to the mouth > shotgun on a
  stand just out of frame > phone in shirt pocket as recorder > camera mic (last resort).
- Room treatment: soft furnishings beat foam panels; avoid bare-wall echo boxes.
- Record 5 seconds of room tone for the editor. Watch levels: peaks around
  -12 to -6 dB, never clipping.

## Sound design & music (plan at production time)

- Choose music per section by function: neutral bed for teaching, rising energy
  for montages, silence as a spotlight for the biggest moment (dropping music out
  is the strongest emphasis tool available).
- Flag license requirements on every suggestion; prefer license-safe libraries
  the creator already pays for.
- SFX list: whooshes/pops for graphics, ambient sound for b-roll realism, UI
  sounds for screen content — subtle, mixed low.

## Art direction

- **Color palette**: pick 2–3 brand hues that also serve thumbnails; keep set
  props and graphics inside the palette.
- **Wardrobe**: solid mid-tone colors that contrast the background; avoid fine
  stripes (moiré) and logo clutter; keep a consistent "uniform" for recognizability.
- **Set/props**: depth over decoration — a few lit background elements beat a
  busy wall. Props that pay off in the script earn their place; others are clutter.

## AI-first creators

If the creator generates assets with AI (Higgsfield, Google Flow/Veo, Runway,
Kling, Midjourney-class tools), the shot list below becomes a **generation
list**: deliver `assets/ai-asset-prompt-pack.md` filled in — a paste-ready
prompt for every visual beat, with a style anchor and consistency plan — per
the AI-first section of `ai-toolkit.md`. Physical direction in this file still
applies to any real A-cam/talking-head footage they mix in.

## B-roll shot list

Turn the script's VISUAL column into a numbered shot list grouped by location/setup
(shoot out-of-order, grouped by setup, to save hours). Each entry:
`# | Shot description | Size/angle | Movement | Script section it covers | Priority (must-have / nice)`.
Overshoot b-roll 2–3× what the edit needs — cutaways are the editor's escape hatches.

## Directing the on-camera performance

- Energy on camera reads ~20% flatter than it feels: direct the creator to
  deliver "one notch more" than natural, without becoming someone else.
- Record in short takes per paragraph rather than full run-throughs; flubs stop
  mattering and energy stays high.
- First line of the video: record it last, when warmed up — it's the most
  important delivery in the piece.
- Eye line: lens = talking to the viewer; slightly off = interview feel. Pick one
  per format and stay consistent.

## Production output format

Deliver: gear/setup summary, per-section coverage plan, b-roll shot list,
lighting diagram description, audio plan, and the `assets/production-checklist.md`
filled in for this shoot.
