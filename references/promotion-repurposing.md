# Phase 8: Promotion & Repurposing

Rule one: **repurpose natively, never cross-post raw.** Each platform gets content
reshaped to its culture, format, and discovery mechanics (specs in `platforms.md`).
A promotion package is not twelve links to the video — it's twelve pieces of
standalone value that make the video the natural next step.

Use `assets/promotion-package-template.md` to assemble the launch kit.

## The repurposing map (from one long-form video)

- **3–5 vertical clips** (Shorts/Reels/TikTok): pick self-contained peaks — a
  surprising claim, the best tip, the funniest moment. Each clip gets its own
  hook line as on-screen text in second one; never open with the long-form intro.
  Recut, don't crop blindly: reframe for 9:16, tighten pacing ~20%.
- **X/Threads thread**: the video's core insights as 5–8 standalone posts;
  first post is a hook (claim or result, no link); the video link goes in the
  final post or a reply — value first, link second.
- **LinkedIn post**: professional-angle rewrite — lesson learned, process, or
  data; first two lines must survive the "see more" fold; native video or
  document-carousel outperforms external links.
- **Instagram carousel**: 6–10 slides; slide 1 = hook (big text, high contrast),
  one idea per slide, final slide = CTA. Carousels earn saves — design for
  "I'll need this later."
- **Facebook**: native video upload or discussion-framed post into relevant
  groups (only where the creator genuinely participates; drive-by link drops
  get removed and hurt reputation).
- **Pinterest**: 2:3 idea pins for evergreen how-to/visual niches; keyword-rich
  pin titles — Pinterest is a search engine with long content half-life.
- **Newsletter**: standalone-value email (see `publishing-seo.md`).
- **Blog article**: expanded, search-optimized text version (see `publishing-seo.md`).
- **Podcast feed**: audio-first edit if the content survives without visuals
  (re-record visual references as spoken descriptions).
- **Community/Discord/Reddit**: behind-the-scenes, an AMA-style discussion
  prompt, or the raw data/resource from the video. On Reddit: follow subreddit
  self-promo rules strictly, give the full value in the post itself, and only
  link when rules and karma allow — Reddit rewards generosity and bans marketing.

## Launch sequencing (first 48 hours)

1. **T-1 day**: community post/story teaser (curiosity, no link yet).
2. **T-0**: publish; pin comment; newsletter + Discord within the first hour
   (early engaged viewers improve early signals AND they're the superfans who
   deserve first access).
3. **T+2–6h**: first vertical clip on Shorts/Reels/TikTok.
4. **T+1 day**: thread/LinkedIn/carousel — the "idea spread" layer.
5. **T+2 days**: second clip; blog article live; Pinterest pins.
6. Reply to every substantive comment in the first 24–48h — comment replies
   compound engagement and train the audience that commenting is worth it.

Space clips over days, not hours: each is a fresh discovery surface, and stacking
them same-day wastes shots at the feed.

## Writing promotional copy

- Every caption is written natively: platform tone (TikTok casual, LinkedIn
  professional, X punchy), platform length, platform hashtag norms
  (counts and placement in `platforms.md`).
- Hooks for promo posts use the same mechanisms as titles (`packaging.md`) —
  curiosity, specificity, stakes — compressed to one line.
- Never write "New video is up!" — state the value or the story, let the video
  be the obvious next click.
- Run the humanizer pass from `scriptwriting.md` on every caption and post:
  AI-tell vocabulary and constructions get posts scrolled past (and quietly
  brand the account as automated) faster than any algorithm penalty.
- Include per-post: copy, hashtag set, visual asset spec (what image/clip),
  and suggested posting time.

## Evergreen re-promotion

Winning content gets promoted more than once. Build a 30/90-day re-promotion
note into the package: re-clip a different moment, re-thread a different insight,
refresh the Pinterest pin, feature it in a later video. Most creators under-promote
their best work and over-produce new work; say this when relevant.
